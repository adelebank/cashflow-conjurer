# The Cashflow Conjurer

A spooky, pixel-art personal-finance game set in an alchemist's hall. Gold is power, debts are ghosts, and the tax wizards are coming. Manage your income statement and balance sheet, invest through the market ticker, and try to escape the Rat Race before you starve.

## How to play

- The game starts on the **2D map**: **WASD** or the arrow keys walk, and you can click a spot or person to walk there
- Press **V** for the optional **3D first-person view**: **W/S** or **Up/Down** walk, **A/D** or **Left/Right** turn, drag the view to look around. A small minimap sits in the corner, and labels only appear for things close to you. Every area is built as a 3D pixel world in the same layout as its 2D map
- On phones and tablets, use the **thumb stick** (bottom left of the view) to walk and turn, drag anywhere else to look, tap a spot or person to walk there, and tap the big **E** button to interact
- Press **V** again to go back to the 2D map (the choice is remembered), and **C** in 3D for an over-the-shoulder camera
- Press **E** to interact
- The **quest bar** above the map always shows your next step (the gold arrow points to it) and this month's checklist: work your shift, pay down debt, check on your business, find 3 glowing coins, then sleep. Each tick gets a cheer
- Tick off everything before you sleep for a **perfect month**: +5 self-esteem and a growing streak
- Sleep on the **red cushion** to end each month
- Grow your passive income until it covers your expenses to escape the Rat Race
- Press **L** for the ledger, **G** for your goal, **N** for the world map, **R** to fly, **K** for your cat, **J** for the story so far, **H** for help

### Your home and your broom

To win a level you must own **a home** and **a broom**. Both are sold in the **Alchemist Hall** under big HOMES and BROOMS signs (Hestia by the left wall, Whisk by the back wall), and better ones cost more:

- **Hestia the Home Seller** sells the Mossy Hut ($2,000), Stone Cottage ($5,000), Crooked Tower ($12,000) and Moonlit Manor ($25,000). Owning a home ends your rent, so you pay a smaller monthly upkeep instead. Bigger homes cost more to keep but lift your self-esteem every month (confident wizards earn 5% more), and from the Cottage up you wake up fed. A home is where you live, not an asset. Trade in your old home for 70% of its price
- **Whisk the Broom Maker** sells the Twig Broom ($300), Oak Sweeper ($900), Comet Broom ($2,400) and Starlight Racer ($6,000). Faster brooms get you there sooner, and the Comet and Starlight have a charm that glows red near tall trees. Trade in your old broom for half its price

Once you own a home, its front door stands in the **Spooky Yard, at the top right next to the Business Office** (out through the hall's Outside Gate, or fly there). The game tells you when you buy it, the NEXT line takes you there for your first visit, and at the end of each month it points you home to sleep. Walk up and press E to go in. Inside you can sleep in your own bed (it ends the month), eat from your pantry and go back out the front door. Bigger homes are bigger inside: the Cottage has a hearth to warm up by, the Tower a telescope for stargazing, and the Manor a library, a chandelier and Bartholomew the ghost butler (each of these lifts your self-esteem once a month).

Press **R** (or **Fly**) to take off. You fly in **first person**, holding your broom, over the whole realm: steer with A/D or the arrow keys (or hold the mouse or your finger to the left or right), W to speed up, S to slow down, and **Space** to look straight down at the map below. Beams of light mark each realm, with its distance, and a minimap shows where you are. Fly into a glowing ring and press **E** to land in that realm (Alchemist Hall, Spooky Yard, Enchanted Forest, Crystal Caverns, Moonlit Harbor, Shadow Gaol, or your own home), or press Esc to turn back. Other wizards fly too, and they will bump you if you get in their way. Fly into a **tall tree** and the screen goes black: you open your eyes in a hospital bed, looking past your blanket at Healer Mirabel, who charges a lot of coins for your recovery ($300 to $750, more for faster brooms). Anything you can't pay goes on your debts, and you wake up at home. Without WebGL, flying falls back to a top-down view.

### Your cat

A little cat adopts you at the start of every game and follows you everywhere: around every realm, into your home, on your broom when you fly, and onto your hospital blanket if you crash. It meows now and then (and hisses at tax wizards). Press **K** (or **Cat**) to name it, pet it (+1 self-esteem once a month), or **upgrade it to a Moon Familiar for $700**: a glowing black cat with starry eyes and a gold collar that runs off to fetch glowing coins for you.

### Your wizard job

At the start of each run you spin the **Wheel of Jobs**. It picks one of eight wizard jobs (Potion Brewer, Dragon Keeper, Owl Post Courier, Rune Scribe, Ghost Exorcist, Crystal Ball Seer, Goblin Bank Teller or Broom Racer), and your job sets your monthly wage. Every job has a **workplace** in the world (for example the Brewing Station in the hall, the Dragon Pen in the forest, the Owl Post Office at the harbor or the Fortune Booth at the gaol), marked by a beam of light. **Go there once a month and press E to work your shift**, a 30-second mini-game that matches your job. No work, no wage: if you sleep without working, you get no wage that month (half in Kids mode), and the game warns you first. A good shift also earns a bonus of up to $300. Press **B** (or **Go to work**) to be reminded where your job is. Brew recipes in order, catch dragon meat or vault coins, fly owl post or race brooms between towers, copy runes, banish ghosts, or follow the crystal through the cup shuffle.

### The townsfolk

The people of the town remember you. They greet you by your profile name, notice when you're hungry, in debt or away for months, ask how the thing you bought from them is doing, and the cooks learn your usual order. Ask anyone **How are you?** to hear their news: each person has a small story that moves on one step each month, and many of the stories are linked (Bram and Old Nan's stew feud, Grim and Madame Levy's tea). People also call out as you walk past, passers-by stop to look at you and gossip about what you own, and the header of each conversation shows how well they know you.

### Playing with friends

When multiplayer is set up, you sign in on the title screen with a **name and password** (press **New player** the first time) or with Google, and pick the nickname your friends will see (never your real name or email). Name accounts need no email, so there is no way to reset a forgotten password: write it down. Everyone gets their own world; press **F** (or **Friends**) to copy your invite link or type a friend's 6-letter code. Friends in the same world see each other walking around, each with their own gold and game, and can talk in the **group chat** under the map (messages also pop up as speech bubbles).

Keeping it kind:
- Mean messages ("you are so stupid", "kys", ...) are blocked before they send, rude words are starred out, and contact details, links and addresses can't be shared
- Every message has a **Report** button, and every player can be reported from the Friends panel. A reported message disappears for everyone straight away
- The moderator reviews reports on `mod.html` and can ban a player. A banned account can't play the game any more
- **Kids mode has no chat**

### The Moonlight Ball

Every so often an owl brings an invitation to the Mayor's ball. Go, and you walk into a real ballroom with chandeliers, a glowing dance floor, musicians, guests to talk to and a waltz. Make your impression by walking up to the Pie Table ($10), the Wine Steward ($40) or the Tavern Bar ($150), or by mingling empty-handed. Dancing is free. Leave through the Grand Doors to head home.

### Languages

The game is available in English, Español, Français, Deutsch, Português and 中文 (Simplified Chinese). Pick a language on the title screen; the choice is remembered in your browser. On a first visit the game uses your browser's language if it is one of these.

### Kids mode

Turn on **Kids mode** on the "Choose your level" screen for gentler rules on any level: 50% more starting gold, 50% more months, half-size starting debts, slower hunger (you can't starve), no tax wizards or Shadow Gaol, no market crashes, scams, layoffs or debt interest, cheaper emergencies, and a plain-language "Kid tip" after each card. The setting is remembered in your browser and saved with each run.

### Story

Before the story begins you were a junior wizard at the Guild of Practical Enchantment, paid a wizard's wage to un-curse other people's teacups and spending every coin of it. Great-Aunt Ysolde visited only once, turned your copper penny into a pile, and told you: "Never spend the penny. Spend what the penny makes." You spent it on a sugar mouse. She wrote your name in her ledger anyway.

Now you have inherited the Alchemist Hall from your Great-Aunt Ysolde, along with her goblin banker, her ledger, and a letter from the Ministry of Dues. The tax wizards would like the hall. Each level opens a chapter of the story, and winning or losing a level adds an epilogue:

1. **The Drafty Hall**: prove to the goblin you can keep coins in a pocket
2. **The Goblin's Wager**: build income that arrives while you sleep and earn the forest map
3. **The Wizards Come Knocking**: Form 7-B brings interest, crashes and the Shadow Gaol
4. **The Siege of Forms**: the Grand Auditor demands the hall sustain itself, in writing
5. **After the Rat Race**: the hall is yours; answer your aunt's last question at your own pace

Starting a level goes straight to a short goal card so you can play right away. Press **J** at any time to read the current chapter.

### Levels and endless mode

Levels 1 to 4 each have a fixed goal and a month limit. **Level 5: Endless Conjurer** has no month limit: you pick your own goal (passive income, net worth, gold in hand, assets owned, or debt free) and a starting map. Reaching a goal earns a trophy and lets you set a bigger one, or retire and see your legend.

After winning or losing any other level you can choose **Keep playing this map (endless)** to carry on with the same run in endless mode.

### Maps

Six areas connect in two loops. Walk to a marked exit and press E (in endless mode the world map also offers fast travel).

- **Alchemist Hall**: your home, the kitchen, the Goblin Bank and the red cushion. Exits: the Outside Gate to the yard, the Harbor Stairs down to the harbor
- **Spooky Yard**: market stalls, Trading Post, Tax Office and Business Office. Exits: the Hall Gate, the Forest Path, the Gaol Road
- **Enchanted Forest**: a treehouse to sleep in, the Druid's Hollow (bank), real-estate and bond stalls, a Berry Cart and the Faerie Ring (trading). Exits: the Yard Path, the Old Mine Shaft down to the caverns
- **Crystal Caverns**: a bedroll by the campfire, the Ore Vault (bank), mine-share and business stalls, a Stew Pot and the Gem Exchange (trading). Exits: the Ladder Up to the harbor, the Forest Tunnel
- **Moonlit Harbor**: a hammock to sleep in, the Harbor Vault (bank), stock and business stalls, a Fish Stall and the Smuggler's Market (trading). Exits: the Hall Stairs, the Cave Mouth, the Prison Hulk
- **Shadow Gaol**: a rain-soaked prison yard where the tax wizards keep debtors. A cell cot to sleep on, the Warden's Vault (bank), Repossessions (real estate) and Bail Bonds stalls, a Gruel Pot and the Debtors' Auction (trading). Exits: the Gaol Road up to the yard, the Prison Hulk down to the harbor

If the tax wizards catch you while you owe overdue debt, they seize up to $250 and drag you to the Shadow Gaol.

The ledger (L) includes a Fortune chart of your net worth by month.

## Multiplayer setup

Multiplayer runs on [Supabase](https://supabase.com) (free tier). Until it is set up, the game plays exactly as before: solo, with no sign-in.

1. Create a Supabase project.
2. In **SQL Editor**, paste and run [`supabase/schema.sql`](supabase/schema.sql). It creates the tables, the security rules, reporting and banning.
3. Name and password sign-in: in Supabase go to **Authentication > Sign In / Providers > Email**, keep it on and turn **off** "Confirm email". Players pick a name, and the game stores it as `<name>@players.cashflow-conjurer.app` (set in `MP_NAME_DOMAIN`). No email is ever sent.
4. Google sign-in (optional; set `MP_GOOGLE=false` in `index.html` to hide the button): in the [Google Cloud console](https://console.cloud.google.com/apis/credentials) create an **OAuth client ID** (type "Web application") and add `https://<your-project>.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback` as an authorized redirect URI. Then in Supabase go to **Authentication > Sign In / Providers > Google**, turn it on and paste the client ID and secret.
5. In Supabase **Authentication > URL Configuration**, set the Site URL to your GitHub Pages address (`https://<username>.github.io/cashflow-conjurer/`) and add it, plus `http://localhost:5173/` if you test locally, to the redirect URLs.
6. Copy the **Project URL** and **anon public key** from **Project Settings > API** into `MP_URL` and `MP_KEY` in both `index.html` (search for `const MP_URL`) and `mod.html`. The anon key is safe to publish; the database rules protect the data.
7. Push to `main`. Sign in to the game once with your own account, then make yourself the moderator by running one of these in the SQL Editor:
   ```sql
   -- a name and password account
   insert into public.admins (user_id) select id from auth.users where raw_user_meta_data->>'username' = 'YourName';
   -- a Google account
   insert into public.admins (user_id) select id from auth.users where email = 'you@example.com';
   ```
8. Open `https://<username>.github.io/cashflow-conjurer/mod.html` to review reports. Ban, dismiss (the message comes back) or unban from there.

Google sign-in does not work from a `file://` page, so test it on the deployed site or a local web server.

## Tech stack

- [TypeScript](https://www.typescriptlang.org/)
- [React](https://react.dev/)
- [Vite](https://vite.dev/)
- [Node.js](https://nodejs.org/) (LTS recommended)

## Getting started

### Prerequisites

- Node.js 20+ and npm

### Install

```bash
npm install
```

### Develop

```bash
npm run dev
```

Then open the URL printed in the terminal (usually http://localhost:5173).

### Build

```bash
npm run build
```

The production build is written to `dist/`.

### Preview the production build

```bash
npm run preview
```

## Project structure

```
.
├── index.html      # The game
├── mod.html        # Moderator page for chat reports and bans
├── supabase/       # Multiplayer database setup (schema.sql)
├── src/            # Application source (TypeScript + React)
├── public/         # Static assets served as-is
└── dist/           # Production build output (generated)
```

## Deployment

Every push to `main` deploys the site to GitHub Pages via `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`, published at `https://<username>.github.io/cashflow-conjurer/`.

One-time setup: in the GitHub repo, go to **Settings → Pages** and set **Source** to **GitHub Actions**.

## Environment variables

Copy `.env.example` to `.env` and fill in any values. Vite only exposes variables prefixed with `VITE_` to client code.
